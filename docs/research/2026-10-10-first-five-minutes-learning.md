# First five minutes of Glass Table

Research date: October 10, 2026. Native baseline: 1.0 (19), source a3db5fc plus documentation 65faf87. Owner feedback: `.scratch/first-five-minutes/feedback.md`. This is a research/prototype delivery, not a native release.

## Direction

Recommend a brief welcome that names the immediate benefit, followed by one taught rule and a simple decision. Offer “Show me first” alongside two answers. Explain every committed choice; show a different example before claiming understanding. Ask about familiarity later, only when it changes the next useful suggestion. A conversational explanation before the existing self-report is a conservative alternative.

Optimize voluntary return because learning feels useful and achievable, rather than maximum screen time. In this offline app, do not add analytics, notification pressure, public help badges or variable reward currency merely to test this idea. A meaningful stopping point is a successful outcome.

## Evidence → application → limits

1. **Competence and autonomy.** Bureau et al. (2022), a meta-analysis of144 studies and >79,000 students, found competence most strongly predicted self-determined motivation, followed by autonomy and relatedness. Application: attainable decision, clear explanation, optional next step. Limitation: educational populations and predictive synthesis; no proof of this app's onboarding or causal retention lift. [Original review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35330866/).
2. **Mastery experience.** Bandura, Adams & Beyer (1977) found mastery-based treatment produced stronger efficacy expectations than observational treatment in adults with phobias. Application: an earned small decision, not praise alone. Limitation: clinical behavioral task, far from casual poker; mechanism informs a hypothesis, not an effect estimate. [Original experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15093/).
3. **Worked examples.** Sweller & Cooper (1985) studied five algebra experiments; example study can reduce unguided search for novices. Application: show what a pair means before asking which hand wins; explain the exact highlighted feature. Limitation: algebra, not poker; guidance may be redundant for experts. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3).
4. **Retrieval.** Roediger & Karpicke (2006) found testing improved delayed retention of prose relative to repeated study, while study could perform better immediately. Application: a fresh decision after instruction, not five passive slides. Limitation: retention, not engagement; do not equate a guessed tap with understanding. [Original experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/).
5. **Feedback.** Wisniewski et al. (2020) synthesized435 studies; effects varied with feedback information. Application: identify the pair and why it beats high card; never just “Correct.” Limitation: educational feedback varies substantially; praise and task explanation are not interchangeable. [Original meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32038429/).
6. **Progress.** Harkin et al. (2016) synthesized138 randomized studies with19,951 participants; progress monitoring promoted goal attainment (average d=.40). Application: show actual completed examples and practiced rules, separately from demonstrated recall. Limitation: goal attainment across domains; does not prove streaks improve this app's retention. [Original review and manuscript](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/).
7. **Praise is conditional.** Henderlong & Lepper (2002) found praise can help, hurt or have no effect depending on sincerity, competence/autonomy support and social comparisons. Application: “That's right—the two aces make a pair,” not “You're a poker genius.” Limitation: primarily children; adult EN/KO tone needs direct testing. [Original review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12206194/).
8. **Mobile evidence cautions against badges as a shortcut.** Baars et al. (2022), a five-week student study-app investigation, found gamification associated with greater autonomous motivation but not higher use frequency/duration or performance. Application: test learning value and comfortable return, not decorate a weak lesson. Limitation: one course app and population. [Original study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.793002/full).
9. **Habits vary.** Lally et al. (2010) followed96 volunteers repeating everyday behaviors in a stable context; modeled habit formation varied substantially, and missing one opportunity did not materially affect the process. Application: easy re-entry and repeatable small practice; missed days should not erase achievements. Limitation: eating/drinking/activity habits, not mobile learning. No universal21-day promise. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674).
10. **Interruption costs.** Stothart et al. (2015) found phone notifications disrupted an attention-demanding laboratory task even without phone interaction. Application: no unnecessary interruptive reward overlays during reasoning. Limitation: not a universal attention-span measurement. The reviewed sources give no valid universal eight-second cap or optimum sentence length. [Original experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121498/).
11. **Apple onboarding.** Apple recommends fast, fun, optional onboarding and instruction near the relevant interface. Application: teach with cards and choices, preserve an exit. This is platform guidance, not experimental psychology. [Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding), [game onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/onboarding-for-games/).
12. **Apple controls and motion.** Apple recommends adequate hit regions, consistent color roles and realistic feedback motion. Application: keep54 pt primary /50 pt secondary baseline; compare52/60 pt answer targets, maintain≥44 pt hit regions; preserve Reduce Motion. Exact timing/color alternatives are design trials, not retention evidence. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion).

## Copy hypotheses

Use one idea per sentence and an action-naming CTA. For an initial375–390 pt portrait test, try short headings and a2–3 line body; roughly8–16 English words per sentence is an editorial target, not a neurological limit. Korean uses natural phrasing and word boundaries, not an English word-count conversion. Match normal EN/KO line groups where natural; allow large-text reflow without shrinking.

- Orientation: “Try one simple hand. We'll explain each choice.” / “쉬운 패 하나부터 볼까요? 선택마다 이유를 알려드릴게요.”
- Familiarity rationale: “To choose a comfortable start, how familiar is poker?” / “편하게 시작할 수 있도록 물어볼게요. 포커가 얼마나 익숙한가요?”
- Correct: “That's right. Two aces make a pair, which beats high card.” / “맞았어요. 에이스 두 장은 페어예요. 페어는 하이 카드보다 높아요.”
- Incorrect: “Look at the two aces. Together they make a pair.” / “에이스 두 장을 볼까요? 같은 숫자 두 장이 페어예요.”
- Help: “Let's look together. Two cards of one rank make a pair.” / “함께 볼게요. 같은 숫자 두 장이면 페어예요.”
- Repeat difficulty: “Let's use a simpler example. You can keep the explanation open.” / “더 간단한 예시로 볼까요? 설명을 계속 열어둘 수 있어요.”
- Factual progress: “2 examples completed · pairs explored” / “예시2개 완료 · 페어 알아보기”. Do not call this mastery or an improvement trend.
- Return: “Welcome back. Pick up where you left off.” / “다시 오셨네요. 이어서 해볼까요?” Use only when a saved activity actually exists; otherwise offer a new example.

## Test before native changes

Compare experience-first versus explained self-report with independent adult poker beginners in EN and KO. Let people use their own pace without coaching or speed targets. Observe first useful action, terminology confusion, mis-taps, explanation in their own words, correct/wrong/help comfort, ability to stop and willingness to return. Check a changed deal immediately and again after24–48 hours; never infer learning from session duration or self-rated enjoyment alone. Use researcher notes with consent; keep product offline/no analytics. Small usability rounds reveal problems, not statistically established retention lifts.

No tested claim that a palette, button size, phrasing or animation makes Glass Table addictive. Candidate B is a reasoned recommendation, not a proven winner. Native progress/resume changes would require separate persistence-safe implementation and verification.
