One voice, different moments

The app should sound like a calm, capable teacher talking to an adult. It can be welcoming at entry, specific after a success, encouraging after a misconception and strictly factual during a reset. In Korean, natural 해요체 often omits the subject; translating every “you” into 당신 would sound unnatural.

Native graded feedback already separates “Matches the chart” from “In this model.” Preserve that distinction; this is a guardrail for the broader redesign, not a newly discovered generic-correctness defect.

Keep the promise consistent after the first five minutes

Less text, enough truth

Lead feedback with one takeaway and a concrete example. Make numerical derivation and advanced terminology expandable. Keep the grade’s basis in a short visible line rather than burying it behind a generic “More.”

Encouragement that earns trust

Compliment a recognized rule or recorded milestone. Never invent “you’re improving” from a single correct guess. A completion count can encourage without declaring mastery. Help remains a valid way to learn.

What I like, what I would refine, what needs testing

Keep

  • Warm, restrained identity with clear card ownership.
  • Separate game and lesson modes.
  • Equal answer emphasis and optional chart access.
  • Factual progress and open topic choice.
  • Compact sound/haptic and appearance preferences.

Refine

  • The abrupt familiarity handoff.
  • Opening-specific visual tokens that drift from native.
  • Custom Previous/Skip press feedback.
  • Help naming and return-path predictability.
  • Feedback hierarchy and different meanings of correctness.

Test before deciding

  • Whether beginners can explain the first rule.
  • Whether chart access feels useful without overwhelming.
  • Thumb comfort and dock clearance on a mini.
  • Partial onboarding resume and experienced-user bypass.
  • EN/KO wording, large text, VoiceOver and physical haptics.

Spacing and heading differences can be intentional. We should standardize their roles and test the result, rather than flatten the entire app into one arrangement or treat every local value as an error.

Recommended decisions, now with app-wide consequences

Keep experience-first as the direction.

It fulfills the approved opening goal. Optional familiarity comes after useful learning; experienced people retain a clear lesson route.

Adopt native-aligned visuals for the opening.

Keep Pretendard, warm adaptive surfaces, amber and current component geometry. This is the smallest visual compromise with the rest of the app.

Do not globally enlarge buttons to 60.

Keep native minimums and matched sibling groups. Compare a larger Continue only on sparse screens; do not steal table/chart space.

Keep bottom task actions and stable root tabs.

Use visible Back/Close according to scope. Help returns to its source without clearing choices; large text can scroll the whole task.

Share a feedback structure, not one generic verdict.

Rule answer, chart match, modeled EV and game outcome need different labels and basis. Shortening must preserve that trust.

Preserve public help-neutral progress.

No with-help/without-help badge or separate public achievement. Factual activity and completion can remain encouraging.

Define the two-example checkpoint separately from five-guide completion.

Its visual recap is approved as a direction. Persisted routing/completion behavior is a later specification and acceptance task, not a new schema decision here.

Unify help discoverability and return behavior.

Retain context-specific containers. A rule explanation, a chart reference and a multi-step tutorial need different lengths and layouts.

Make bilingual consistency a component acceptance rule.

Match intended normal line groups with natural word boundaries. Allow accessibility reflow instead of forcing identical large-text screenshots.

Keep all implementation out of this exploration.

No new build number or phone update. These decisions guide the next design iteration and beginner testing; they do not claim native acceptance.

No new preference question is necessary: these are my recommendations under your approved direction. The report makes the compromises explicit so you can compare them before any app work.

The next evidence we need

  1. Observed comprehension: ask adult beginners to explain why a hand wins, identify what the chart measures and distinguish a chart match from winning the game. Compare languages independently.
  2. Cross-mode predictability: after the opening, ask them to find help in a lesson and graded practice, dismiss it, resume, review a hand and change a setting without coaching.
  3. Physical mini-device comfort: compare 54/60 primary trials, answer reach and body scrolling. Observe slips and effort, not just which screenshot people prefer.
  4. Interrupted-session definitions: specify returning after one/two/five comparisons, optional placement, replay, lesson draft and mid-game exit. Preserve current progress and recovery bytes.
  5. Later native matrix: EN/KO × light/dark, normal/AX5, iOS 17/current iOS, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion/Transparency, Increased Contrast, long values and disabled states. Verify actual chart/model feedback and help returns.

Keep the product offline. Manual usability observations can test these ideas without adding analytics, accounts or tracking. A retention experiment is a separate research question and does not follow automatically from this visual study.
