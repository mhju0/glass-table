# Final source audit for device feedback C016–C028

Read-only audit of the current native sources. Source observations below are separate from the user’s live-device reports and proposed design changes. The user ended device testing; this file records research and does not authorize product or device changes.

## C016–C018: Defend lessons, chart help, and example balance

In `GlassTable/Sources/Screens/NodeSessionView.swift:301–335`, the `.together` stage renders a drill as explicitly ungraded practice. The Defend graded screen in `GlassTable/Sources/Screens/ConceptDrillView.swift:2830–2898` shows the hand, opener/action and three choices before submission; `DefendGridView` appears only under `if reveal != nil` (`:2856–2864`). The guided script introduces the opener’s range, derives the response bands, and then shows the chart in `GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/Beats.swift:625–652` and `Localization/EnglishBeatScript.swift:342–357`.

This supports teaching with a visible chart in the ungraded guided stage. On a graded decision, showing the chart with the current hand highlighted before commitment reveals the answer. An unhighlighted chart could be offered as help, but the app would need to disclose and preserve the distinction between helped and unassisted evidence internally. Do not publish a helped-versus-solo performance category; existing public practice summaries combine these.

Current chart evidence is tested after reveal: `GlassTable/Tests/LearningFlowTests.swift:683–700` and `AccessibilityFlowTests.swift:295–315`. The screenshot supplies the visual observation of the dark Defend header crowding; source shows a top Hint control, inline cue and drill title/progress at `NodeSessionView.swift` and `ConceptDrillView.swift:375–424,451–464`, but source alone does not establish pixel overlap.

`DefendSpotGenerator.spot` shuffles candidate hands and opener and filters Chen score to 2…12 (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/DefendDrill.swift:24–41`). It does not balance correct actions. The first visit uses worked/together indexes 0/1 and first graded spots 2–4 (`NodeSessionView.swift:289–310,375–380`). `DefendChart.swift:17–43` maps top 30% to raise again, through 75% to call, and the remainder to fold. Tests establish deterministic generation, the Chen range, and all three actions somewhere among 80 generated spots, but no balance among the first three (`GlassTableDrills/Tests/GlassTableDrillsTests/DefendDrillTests.swift:28–55`).

A curated, deterministic fold/call/raise sequence can teach the three response categories in the ungraded example, with randomized presentation order. Keep the graded sampler and grader unchanged unless a versioned saved-session plan is designed. Saved answer validation regenerates the original spot from seed/index (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/ProgressionStore.swift:353+`, `Progression/SavedDrillAnswer.swift`); active replay identity must remain stable.

## C019: Learning order and “Which cards turn it around?”

The current required sequence is hand comparison and pot math, then position/RFI/Defend, then outs/equity/pot odds (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/LearningTrack.swift:27–35`). First-run basics follows the FirstLesson (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/RootView.swift:69–78`); it begins with a pair comparison and optional rank ladder (`GlassTable/Sources/HoldemBasicsContent.swift:10–18`). The user’s idea that math may cause disengagement is a hypothesis; no dropout measure was found.

Outs are strict river wins against a known opponent: `countOuts` includes only river cards where hero’s evaluated seven-card hand is greater than villain’s and excludes ties (`GlassTableEngine/Sources/GlassTableEngine/Outs.swift:5–18`). The graded prompt requests a numeric count (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/ConceptDrillView.swift:1466–1480`); the reveal later introduces rule-of-2 percentage (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/Beats.swift:194–197`, `Localization/EnglishBeatScript.swift:80–82`). A potential teaching progression is visual hand outcome first, then count, then optional percentage. Pot math already asks the basic current-pot count at indexes 0–2; fractional arithmetic can appear later (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/PotMath.swift:232–259`, `ConceptDrillView.swift:836–842`). These are presentation proposals only; preserve current three-question completion and grading.

| Pot math | Three generated chip-value choices | `ConceptDrillView.swift:958–991` |

| Outs, combos, range notation | Integer entry | `ConceptDrillView.swift:1477,1771`; `DesignSystem/CountEntryView.swift` |

| Equity sense, EV call | Point slider plus interval-width slider/buttons + check | `ConceptDrillView.swift:598–647,1322–1325,1404–1407` |

| Hit frequency, range advantage, action read | Point and 90% interval controls + check | `ConceptDrillView.swift:2333–2336,2462–2469,2810–2816` |

`Concept.isEstimation` marks `equitySense`, `evCall`, `outs`, `hitFrequency`, `rangeAdvantage`, and `actionRead` (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/Concept.swift:21–38`), but `outs` is a documented exception: that flag represents its equity half only. The shipped outs question asks for an integer out count and is graded as an exact/near count, not a continuous point-plus-interval estimate or Winkler-calibration evidence (`Concept.swift:24–35`; `GlassTable/Sources/Screens/ConceptDrillView.swift:1466–1480`; `GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Reveal.swift:14–20`). For the other estimation drills, answer data retain continuous point/low/high intervals (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Estimation.swift:6–16`, `Progression/LearningProgress.swift:79–82`, `Progression/SavedDrillAnswer.swift:8–16`). RangeRead is also an estimate but uses width/shape overlap, not interval calibration (`Concept.swift:24–35`).

One-tap choices are a natural fit for decisions and can reduce input effort. Converting numeric or range lessons to 2–3 choices changes the evidence from error distance, confidence interval coverage/Winkler scoring, or range overlap to discrete correctness. Do not present that as the same calibration measure. A beginner-friendly discrete check can coexist with later advanced calibration, but switching formats must not silently change old answer interpretation, grading, calibration history, or saved-question replay; version the format and disclose which measure is being used. Do not add a public assisted/solo performance split.

## C021: Outs outcome grouping (corrected user observation)

The existing reveal already shows the selected river card, both players’ completed hand names, and whether the hero wins or the opponent still wins (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/ConceptDrillView.swift:1513–1535`, `DesignSystem/Components.swift:260–285`). The user corrected the earlier concern: the main request is to organize cards into outcome groups to reduce redundant tapping, while retaining individual access and suit distinctions.

Current `outsGrid` is a flat adaptive grid. Each card remains individually tappable and sets `tappedOut` (`ConceptDrillView.swift:1530–1552`). `HandBrief` only carries category and top rank (`GlassTableEngine/Sources/GlassTableEngine/HandEvaluator.swift:107–119`); grouping on that alone can merge different kickers or opponent comparisons. `evaluate7` exact strength is internal (`:139`), while `bestFiveCards` exposes each player’s actual five-card result (`:187`).

If grouping is pursued, group only cards with an identical complete comparison outcome for both players: equivalent final hand/tiebreak strength and the same winner/tie result. Keep each suit-specific river card individually selectable inside its group, show the group count, and preserve the selected-card comparison including the opponent’s final best five. Do not group solely by “hero improved” or broad hand category; suits can create flushes/straight flushes, kickers affect the winner, and the opponent’s best five may change. The current winning-out set uses strict hero-over-villain comparison, so ties are not part of that grid.

## C022: Repeated example variety and stable saved questions

FirstLesson replays the same two fixed example hands (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/FirstLesson.swift:7–23`); Hold’em Basics uses a fixed pair-of-kings example (`GlassTable/Sources/HoldemBasicsContent.swift:10–18`). ConceptExplain and drill help use the same walkthrough salt `0xE9A1 + seedCount(concept)`, index 0 (`ConceptExplainView.swift:61–66`, `ConceptDrillView.swift:578`). The salt changes as saved attempts/help increment `ProgressionModel.seedCount` (`ProgressionModel.swift:173–176`), so reopening before an increment can repeat the same example.

Node sessions derive a seed from node ID and concept totals and use indexed spots (`NodeSessionView.swift:27–38,145,375–387`). Resuming the same saved session must reproduce the same question; curriculum format 2 schedules three questions while format-1 snapshots retain their older five-question form (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/Curriculum.swift:175–184`). For replay-only guides, a deterministic example pool can provide different spots over successive replays. Do not rotate the spot in an active saved session. If rotation state is persisted, version it; verify guide replays vary while saved active-session replays remain identical.

## C023: Free Table help-tab destinations and clearer names

The tabs are in one dock/back panel, not separate pages. The opening Hint pill selects `.hint`; tabs are rendered in `GlassTable/Sources/Screens/FreeTableView.swift:646–658,1331–1356`, with visible strings KO “힌트 / 이번 핸드 / 규칙”, EN “Hint / This hand / Rules”. They have identifiers `free-back-hint|hand|rules` and selected traits, but no additional destination-specific accessibility hint.

- **Hint** (`FreeTableView.swift:1528–1592`): at a live decision it explains action meanings and shows current facts (pot, call price, needed equity, made hand/start-hand rank), with an optional beginner-chart suggestion behind a second “What would the beginner chart do?” action. At a settled hand it offers review and next-hand/new-table actions (`:1530–1541`). Suggested labels: “선택 도움말” / “Decision help”.
- **This hand** (`:1602–1660`): action history grouped by street, opponent styles, and after settlement the hand review, chip movement/refunds, optional folded cards, and final best five. Suggested labels: “핸드 기록” / “Hand history”.
- **Rules** (`:1702–1750`, `GlassTable/Sources/Screens/PlayView.swift:159–217`): table guide plus expandable computer policy details. Suggested labels: “테이블 안내” / “Table guide”. If explicitly naming bot material fits, “테이블·상대 안내” / “Table & bot guide”.

Optional accessibility hints can state destinations plainly (“See the call price and optional chart suggestion”, “Review actions and chip movement”, “Learn the table and computer choices”). Labels should not imply the chart is optimal or mandatory.

## C025: Quick Play recap, action metrics and hand archive

Quick Game is 40 chips each, ten hands, 1/2 blinds, ending after hand ten or earlier when the learner has no chips (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/TableSetupView.swift:108–110,145–155`; `GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/PracticeTable/PracticeTableState.swift:94–113`). Current per-hand result shows net chips, winner, and at game-over finishing place/chips (`FreeTableView.swift:809–855`). `FreeTableHandCoachView` reviews only the current settled table’s choices and chart verdicts (`FreeTableView.swift:146–150`; `FreeTableHandCoachView.swift:14–50,82–116,138–180`). `previousHand` is transient `@State` used by replay animation, not an archive (`FreeTableView.swift:34,802,925,965`).

Persisted `recentHands` is bounded to 200 records and stores only hand ID, day, rules/policy versions, voluntarily entered, and raised preflop (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/Progression/ProgressState.swift:149,191–219`; `Progression/LearningProgress.swift:267–271`; `PracticeTable/ObservedStyle.swift:4–19`). It does not retain hand cards, board, events, per-action decisions or verdicts. `tableState` keeps only current hand. Therefore all-ten-hand review and action counts cannot be rebuilt after moving to another hand/relaunch; that requires a durable per-hand archive or event summaries with bounded retention/versioning.

If captured prospectively, truthful metrics include hands played, voluntary entries/hands, preflop raises/entered hands, chip change per hand, and event counts for fold/check/call/bet/raise. Label the denominator (e.g. “calls / decisions where call was legal”). Archived `PracticeHandCoach` verdicts can support “matches beginner chart in x/y reviewed decisions” with Good/Fine/Risky counts, explicitly the app’s simplified chart and not optimal poker quality. “Successful bluff” implies intent that action logs cannot establish. `PracticeHandCoach.reveals` only flags certain opponent postflop bets with no pair/draw at showdown (`GlassTableDrills/Sources/GlassTableDrills/PracticeTable/PracticeHandCoach.swift:150–166`); it neither classifies learner intent nor all bets. “Lucky” is also inference; show chip results beside decision review rather than labeling luck. “Risky” should mean the disclosed chart’s label, not financial risk.

`PracticeStyleAnalysis` is separate cross-session evidence: recent 30 days/current bot versions, with minimum 100 hands, five days and 40 voluntary entries (`ObservedStyle.swift:24–60`). It is not a Quick Game score. No public helped/solo comparison should be added.

## C026: Pause versus abandoning a table

The top-left close always closes Free Table’s full-screen presentation (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/FreeTableView.swift:202–210`). The table remains saved in `model.state.tableState`; Play then offers “자유 대전 이어서” / “Continue the free table” (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/PlayView.swift:99–111`). This is pause/resume. `ProgressionModel.startTable` refuses to start a second table while `tableState` is non-nil (`:800–807`). `leaveFinishedTable` only clears a table if its street is finished (`:852–857`). The current confirmation “Start a fresh table?” is attached to finished-hand flow only (`FreeTableView.swift:145–160,873–881`). Thus mid-hand new-game is unavailable, not a close-state bug.

Keep “Back to Play” / “플레이로 돌아가기” as a clear pause route and retain Continue. Add a distinct confirmed “Start new game” / “새 게임 시작” action for abandonment; the confirmation should state that the current unfinished hand/table will be discarded, while already settled records remain. Don’t clear a live saved table silently. Current records contain only summarized settled-hand habits (C025), so do not promise a full replay archive.

## C027: Progress screen density and data-backed visuals

`RecordsView` puts completion/activity and recent topics first, then one collapsed “Detailed learning records” group (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/RecordsView.swift:20–59`). Expanded detail contains headline stats, practice over time, observed table habits, confidence, optional EV loss, and topic rows (`:42–59`). Existing visuals include a seven-day activity indicator and lesson completion count (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/PracticeActivityView.swift:9–52`), per-topic four-stage pips and outcome bar (`RecordsView.swift:195–217,499–546,563–573`), and a confidence meter with 90% target (`:409–453`). The detailed section remains text-heavy by design, but Progress already has graphics.

To make it easier to scan, retain completion-first and group detail into compact labeled cards: Learning path (course completion/topic stages), Practice (today/week exact-near-missed counts with denominator), Confidence (only when interval sample exists), Decisions (EV loss only when present), Table habits (sample-qualified preflop behavior). Use bars/rings only when a meaningful denominator exists and retain accessible text/labels. `PathView.swift:105–134` already provides four track categories with `cleared/total`; it may be the better home for category-level course progress instead of repeating every track in Records.

Concept rows report app tier, total, exact-or-near accuracy and due date (`RecordsView.swift:499–573`); completion is distinct from proficiency (`PracticeActivityView.swift:9–52`, Records caveat `:43–46`). Practice-period comparisons filter by concept/mode/format/language but do not expose assisted/unassisted as public categories (`RecordsView.swift:183–189,265–295`). Keep that privacy/product boundary. Do not infer “improvement” from mastery alone; no lock/pressure styling is needed. Preserve bilingual copy, Dynamic Type stacking and accessibility labels.

## C028: Settings controls and feedback preferences

Current Settings is one scrolling page. Language and appearance occupy the top as two cards with three 48pt rows each; followed by poker terms, daily reminder, learning links, backup/import/reset, support/legal/version (`GlassTable/Sources/Screens/SettingsView.swift:43–132,133–259`). Preferences persist through `@AppStorage` (`:8–9`); keys are `glassTable.language` (`GlassTable/Sources/AppLanguage.swift:5–15`) and `appAppearance` (`DesignSystem/Theme.swift:12–33`). Language/appearance choices are bilingual, selected controls carry accessibility traits and IDs (`SettingsView.swift:53–71,87–105`), and appearance applies to settings and presented sheets (`:262`).

Haptics are currently unconditional and bypass a shared preference: graded reveals call `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator` (`ConceptDrillView.swift:325–330`), FirstLesson (`FirstLessonView.swift:601`), Hold’em Basics (`HoldemBasicsView.swift:265`), and Free Table `.sensoryFeedback` (`FreeTableView.swift:133`, `DesignSystem/TableFeedback.swift:3–21`). The code comment identifies iOS Reduce Haptics as the current opt-out. A user-facing app haptics setting would need one central policy used by all these call sites, while preserving system-level opt-out.

No in-app sound playback source was found. The only sound-related API is local-notification authorization requesting `.sound` in `GlassTable/Sources/DailyReminder.swift:25–31`; that is notification behavior and is not an app sound-effects setting. A sound toggle now would be a no-op and misleading. Keep reminder notification sound separate; add an app sound toggle only if in-app sound effects are introduced.

Compact grouping proposal: Learning (replay, starting point, learning guide, glossary); Play & feedback (daily reminder and haptics if implemented); Data (export/import/reset); Support & legal; collapsed “Language & appearance” lower down with two compact native picker/menu rows showing current values. Honor the user’s preference to move rarely changed language/appearance controls lower on the page. Keep 44pt targets, accessible value/selected state, and test Korean/English, light/dark, AX5. Do not add an in-app sound switch without sound playback.
